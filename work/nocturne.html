Nocturne

You wake in a grey room with someone else's habits in your hands. Every night you choose what to touch — and what to leave alone.

The brief

One week, for a Unity course. I set myself one rule: make a game I hadn't played before.

The endings — spoilers, play first

The endings come from one rule: what you disturb is noticed. Sleep through every night without touching anything and you do not wake up. Disturb the room without being caught and you take the place of the person whose room it is. Get caught, and the person you displaced talks to you. Doing nothing is one of the endings, and SLEEP is the button that leads there.

How it plays

Instead of a list of choices, each night is a map of the room: desk, mirror, bookshelf, a door, a bed — and a cabinet that is locked from the first night. Looking at a place writes a few lines of prose and sometimes opens a new place on the map.

When you have seen enough, you sleep, and the next night begins. There are three endings.

Built around the interface

A menu says 'pick an option'. A map says 'this is a room, and something in it is locked'. The suspense in Nocturne is carried by the layout rather than by the prose: the locked node is visible, labelled and unreachable, every single night.
