The rule nobody wrote down

When you take damage, you choose whether it lands on your own life or the team's — and taking it pays you resources. We played it that way for ten years. It was in neither edition of the rulebook, which is why nobody could learn this game without me in the room.

How it plays

Two teams of two share one deck. On your turn you draw, gain resources and play cards — but only one attack a turn. Defence happens on the other player's turn, through three response windows: defend, counter, rescue.

The game's one rising curve comes from being hit. Damage pays you resources, and you decide whether it costs your own life or the shared team pool. Sacrificing yourself for a teammate is both the kindest and the strongest play on the table.

Revision history

  • 2016
    First edition, on paper

    Designed and hand-made the deck. Rules written for the people already at the table.

  • 2017–2025
    Years of play

    The same group, again and again. Resource cards were cut. A second rulebook fixed the wording and inherited the same blind spot.

  • 2026
    Specification, and what it exposed

    Assembled a four-person team for a PC version, scoped to 2v2 and under thirty minutes. Writing the rules for a programmer surfaced the damage rule and 52 other decisions nobody had ever said out loud.

What I changed, and why

WasA game that worked because the designer was in the room.
NowA spec in which every rule has a trigger, a condition and a result.

The digital version forced the question every tabletop designer can avoid: what exactly happens, in what order? Answering it for a programmer was the first time the game could be taught by something other than me.

The game's own site — characters, factions, trailer — is in the works.
